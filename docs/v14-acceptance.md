# V14 acceptance checks

The build, TypeScript checks and Node regression suite can run without Foundry. They do not establish live world acceptance. A Foundry V14 installation was not available during this update.

Use a V14-compatible PF2e release and enable the current Astillon Hanaq Adventures build. Reload connected clients after installing the build. Menacing and Your Turn do not require Sequencer or JB2A; Blaster's optional integrations have their own [acceptance checks](blaster-acceptance.md).

## Menacing

1. As GM, open a token's standard status palette. Find **Menacing**, click it, and confirm an animated purple symbol appears at the upper-right edge. The status control should highlight. Ordinary conditions such as Frightened must still work.
2. Move, drag, resize, rotate, and zoom around the token. The symbol should follow smoothly and stay upright. Toggle it off and confirm immediate removal. Repeat quickly to check for duplicates.
3. Place two linked tokens of the same actor. Enable Menacing on one; the other must remain unchanged. Actor statistics, conditions, and effects must be unchanged.
4. Connect a player who owns a token. Toggle Menacing from that client; both clients should agree. A user without ownership must not change the flag. Reload a client and switch away from/back to the scene; the animation should return exactly once.
5. Hide the token as GM and test player vision, fog, wall occlusion, detection-only visibility, and V14 scene levels. Players must never see Menacing when its token artwork is not visible. The GM should see a dimmed symbol for a hidden token.
6. Delete a token with Menacing active; no symbol should remain. Switch scenes while assets are loading; no symbol from the old scene should appear on the new scene.
7. Change PF2e's condition icon theme and death icon. Reopen the palette; Menacing should still be present and toggle normally.
8. Tab to Menacing and use Enter/Space. Right-click the status. Each interaction should toggle once, without changing the token's large overlay status.

## Your Turn

Upgrade check: on V14, save differing personal values for the primary GM and a player before updating. After all clients reload, missing world enable/scale/hold choices must use only the GM's values. An already saved world Off must remain Off. With no saved GM preference, use the defaults. Verify secondary GMs and players do not seed the shared settings, and disabling the world toggle cancels loaded/pending artwork and stingers on both GM and player clients.

1. Leave **Your Turn Cut-In** off. Advance initiative. No cut-in should appear. From an actor sheet you can edit, open Your Turn and choose Preview. The draft plays anyway, without enabling the setting, writing flags, or advancing combat. A sheet you cannot edit has no control.
   - Editor Preview must remain fully visible and stationary beyond 2.6 seconds (including reduced-motion clients), with its sound playing only once. Stop preview, Save, Cancel/close, scene teardown, or a replacement combat card must remove it. Direct API preview without an editor remains timed.
   - While it is held, use every arrow, Shift-click, X/Y input, scale slider/number, −/+ and Reset. Only the portrait moves/scales, the same card remains visible, and no new sound or document write occurs. Test negative/multi-digit typing without premature field rewriting. Save/reopen preserves values; Cancel/close and failed Save preserve old values. Verify the result on the next automatic card and another client.
   - Keep two editors for the same actor open: controls/Stop/Save/close in the older editor must not move or dismiss the newer editor's preview or an intervening combat card. Edit placement while artwork is loading/decoding: the eventual preview uses the latest draft. Test token inheritance and explicit Reset overriding a base actor's non-default placement.
2. As a GM with Configure Settings permission, enable the world setting. On the next change of combatant, round, or turn, one cut-in should enter from the lower right of the map for the GM and every player, including players with an old personal opt-out. Players must not see the enable/scale/hold/width controls in Configure Settings or be able to save a world override. A dark jagged wedge, a theme-colored panel, a lighter shard, and white speed lines should be visible behind the portrait. The banner is a black brush shape with a white edge. The large line is the combatant's name and the smaller line is YOUR TURN. The portrait has a thick white outline. Most of the map to the left stays visible and clickable. The cut-in leaves on its own. Another player's combatant should be announced too. Collapse the sidebar or resize the window: the playing graphic stays at its original measured position and does not replay.
3. Save a custom image and color on the actor. That image is used, then the same flag on the token's base actor, then the actor image, then the token image. Clear the image and confirm the actor image is used, then the token image. An invalid color falls back to #2B8CFF. With reduced motion enabled, the cut-in fades in and out together instead of slamming in layers.
4. In the real actor editor, click **Browse**: Foundry's file browser must open, and choosing an image must populate the path without closing the editor. Change the color and **Preview**: the draft plays without saving. **Save**, close, and reopen: both values persist. Repeat with two different actors' editors open and verify neither editor reads or saves the other's fields. Confirm an actual failed save produces a notification.
5. Reload GM and player clients with custom art on a world actor, an unlinked scene token, and a combatant. Observe startup image requests in the browser Network panel, or await `game.astillonHanaqAdventures["your-turn"].preload()`. Advance to those actors' first turns: the portrait and shared slices must be ready for the full animation rather than appearing halfway through. No world documents should be written by preloading.
6. Throttle the network and select a new image. Before it is ready, there must be no empty timed flash-in. Once decoded, its full 2.6-second timeline starts. Advance past that combatant while its image loads, cancel a preview, or leave the scene: no obsolete cut-in may appear later. A broken custom image should fall back without poisoning subsequent valid images. Saving changed artwork on another client should warm it on this client too.
7. As GM, set **Your Turn Portrait Width (px)** to 400, then 1000, then 250. Compare the same art exported at 64px and 1024px: at world scale 1 both must reach the exact selected width, not their intrinsic size or a hidden percentage of the stage. Repeat with transparent and opaque portraits, a wide image, and a tall image. Preserve proportions; tall/large art may extend beyond the backdrop or viewport instead of silently shrinking. Opaque art must not be stretched or cropped to fill an unrelated aspect ratio. Width changes should apply to the next cached preview without new downloads or actor edits. Players receive the world width and scale but do not control them. Invalid values use the default, and numeric widths clamp only to the documented 100–1000 setting range. Transparent padding still counts as part of the image width.

### Your Turn sound follow-up

- Browse beside **Sound stinger** opens the native audio picker without submitting the editor. Choose a short audio file, Preview, and Save. Reopen and verify art, color, and sound persist together. Clear sound and save to return to silence.
- With the GM's world setting enabled, each GM/player viewer hears exactly one local stinger when the portrait appears; disabling the world setting makes automatic playback silent for everyone. Interface volume and global mute remain local. A preview is heard only by the previewing user.
- Test Stop preview, Cancel, window-close, sound-path changes, rapid combat turns, scene teardown, and disabling cut-ins during a pending load. No obsolete or overlapping sound should start later, including after the browser's first audio-unlocking gesture.
- Test slow/broken audio and a clip longer than 2.6 seconds. Portrait playback remains functional; a playing clip must finish naturally after the card disappears, without restarting or cutting off. Stop preview, scene teardown, a replacement cut-in, and world disable must still stop the sound after visual expiry. A sound still waiting for unlock/download at visual expiry must not start belatedly. Closing one actor editor must not stop another editor's newer preview or an automatic combat cut-in.

## Existing content

2. Run Sarsaparilla Surprise on an actor with strikes. Change the selected strike and verify its displayed modifier, select Draw, then test Hit, Stand, and window-close behavior. Confirm the result chat card and card-stack expansion work for GM and player clients.
